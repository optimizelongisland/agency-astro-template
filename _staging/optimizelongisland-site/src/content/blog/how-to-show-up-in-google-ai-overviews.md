You cannot apply for a place in a Google AI Overview, and you cannot pay for one. An AI Overview is an AI-written summary that appears near the top of some Google searches, with links to the sites it drew from. A local business makes itself easier to cite by doing the plain things well:

1. **Pages that answer the question directly.** The answer comes first, in the first sentences, in plain language.
2. **Accurate structured data.** Markup that tells Google what the business is, where it works and what the page's questions and answers are.
3. **A complete Google Business Profile.** Categories, service area, hours, phone number and website, all filled in.
4. **Consistent listings.** The same name, address and phone number everywhere your business is listed.
5. **Pages Google can crawl and index.** If Google cannot read a page, it cannot cite it.

AI Overviews appear on some searches and not others. So the useful question for a Long Island business is not "how do I get one" but "which of my customers' searches show one, and who gets cited when they do?" We check exactly that for clients, and this post explains how.

## What are Google AI Overviews?

A Google AI Overview is a short answer written by AI that usually appears near the top of the results for certain searches. It gives a summary and shows links to the pages it used, so a searcher can click through. Google also offers AI Mode, a separate AI search view. Google's guidance for site owners covers both the same way: there are no extra technical requirements beyond being indexed and eligible to appear in Search.

Google describes these features in its own Search documentation, and that is the place to confirm current details, because the company changes how they work. We are not going to guess at the inner workings. What matters to a business owner is simple: the summary is built from web pages, and the links shown are pages that Google found and judged useful for that search.

People often ask whether this is the same thing as ChatGPT. It is not. ChatGPT is a separate assistant that you open yourself. An AI Overview shows up inside Google Search when you search. Both use AI to write answers, and both lean on pages they can find. ## Can any business show up in an AI Overview?

Any business with a crawlable website can be linked in one, but there is no application and no payment, and many searches do not show an AI Overview at all.

Google's own guidance for site owners says pages need to be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet, and that there is no special markup that ensures inclusion. That is worth taking at face value. The same fundamentals that help you rank in ordinary results are the base for everything else.

Whether a given search shows an AI Overview varies. One search for a service in your town may show one, and a slightly different wording may not. It also changes over time. This is why guesses are not much use and checking is. A search for "plumber in Islip" might show a map pack and normal listings, and "how much does a new water heater cost" might show an AI summary. Only looking at the real results tells you which.

Be cautious of anyone who says they can get you into AI Overviews. They do not control Google.

## What makes a page more likely to be cited?

A page is easier to cite when it gives a clear, self-contained answer to a specific question, backed by plain facts. Google does not publish a formula, so treat this as a checklist of good practice and not a set of ranking factors.

- **Lead with the answer.** The opening paragraph should answer the main question fully, so a reader could stop there.
- **Use question headings.** "How much does a new roof cost on Long Island?" matches how people ask. "Roofing Pricing Considerations" does not.
- **State plain facts.** Prices or price ranges, towns you serve, hours, how long a job takes. Specific facts are easier to quote than general claims.
- **Make each section stand alone.** If someone lifted one section out of the page, it should still make sense.
- **Use a table or numbered list where the content fits.** Steps and comparisons are naturally lists.
- **Add structured data.** It helps search systems read the page in a standard format. It supports good content and does not replace it.
- **Keep it current.** Update prices, hours and services when they change.
- **Name your places.** For a local business, say Suffolk County, Nassau County, the actual towns. Location is part of what makes a result relevant.

## Why are my AI Overviews not showing up?

If your site never appears in AI Overviews, the usual causes are the same ones that keep a site out of ordinary results, and the first one to rule out is that Google cannot read or index the pages.

Work through these in order:

1. **Indexing and crawling.** If the page is not in Google's index, it cannot be cited. Our post on [why your website is not showing up on Google](/blog/why-is-my-website-not-showing-up-on-google/) shows how to check this for free in Google Search Console.
2. **Thin or vague pages.** A page that says "we offer quality service" gives Google nothing to quote. Say what you do, where and how.
3. **No Google Business Profile, or an unfinished one.** For local searches, the profile is a main source of facts about you.
4. **Inconsistent listings.** If your phone number or address differs from one site to the next, Google has less reason to trust any of them.
5. **The search simply does not show one.** Not every search gets an AI Overview. Sometimes there is nothing to fix.
6. **Other sites are stronger sources.** For a given question, Google may prefer a directory, a review site or a competitor with a clearer page.

The fifth and sixth are worth saying out loud. Sometimes the answer is that the page you want to be cited is fine, and the search is one where a different kind of site gets linked. A directory listing, a review profile or a mention elsewhere may do more for you than another page on your own site.

## How do you track AI Overviews for your business?

You track them by looking at the real search results the way a local customer sees them, and doing it the same way each time. Google results differ by location, so a search from your office in West Islip may not match one from a customer in Hauppauge or Garden City.

You can do a rough version yourself. List the 10 to 15 searches your customers use, run each one, and note whether an AI Overview appears and which sites it links to. Repeat monthly and compare.
