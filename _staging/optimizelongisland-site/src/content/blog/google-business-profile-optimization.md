Google Business Profile optimization means filling in and maintaining your free Google listing so it appears for the right searches on Google Maps and in the map results. Here is the short checklist for a Long Island business:

1. Choose the right primary category.
2. List every service you offer.
3. Make sure your name, address or service area, phone number and hours are accurate.
4. Add real photos of your work, your team and your premises.
5. Write a description that says what you do and where.
6. Post regularly.
7. Ask for reviews and reply to every one.
8. Watch your results each month.

## What is a Google Business Profile, and why does it matter on Long Island?

A Google Business Profile is the free listing that shows your business on Google Maps and in Google Search. It carries your name, phone number, hours, website link, photos, reviews and the things you offer. When someone in Huntington or Islip searches for a plumber, a dentist or a restaurant, Google often shows a short list of local businesses with a map above the ordinary results. Your profile is what gets you onto that list.

For a local business, that box can matter as much as the first organic result, because it shows a phone number and directions in one tap.

You also need to claim and verify the profile with Google. Until you do, you cannot control what it says.

## Which category should you choose?

Choose the category that describes your main business as precisely as you can. Your primary category tells Google what you are, and it has a large effect on which searches you appear for. A plumber who does the whole trade, for example, would pick "Plumber".

Google also lets you add secondary categories for other things you do. Use them for real services, not for hopeful ones. A roofer who also does gutters can add gutter service. A roofer who has never done solar should not add solar.

Look at how other businesses in your trade categorize themselves, but do not copy them blindly. Their primary category may not match your work. Then check it again every few months, because Google adds and renames categories.

## What should your services, description and hours say?

Be specific, plain and consistent with your website.

**Services.** List each thing you do, one by one. "Water heater replacement" tells Google and customers more than "plumbing."

**Description.** Say what you do and where, in ordinary sentences. Name the towns you work in, such as West Islip, Babylon or Bay Shore, if they are true. Leave out claims you cannot back up, and avoid filling it with keywords.

**Hours.** Keep regular hours accurate and update holiday and special hours before they happen. A customer who drives to a closed shop will not forget it.

**Name, phone and website.** Use the real name as it appears on your sign and website, a phone number that gets answered, and a link to the page that best fits the profile.

Whatever you put here should match your website. If your website says you serve Suffolk and Nassau counties, your profile should not say something different.

## Do photos and posts help?

Yes, in the way a window display helps a shop. Customers use photos to judge whether you look real and capable before they call.

Use real photos: your work, your trucks or storefront, your team. Avoid stock images. Add new ones as you finish jobs. Photos from your own phone are fine if they are clear and in good light.

Posts are short updates that show on your profile. Use them for offers, completed projects, seasonal reminders and news. In our Local SEO plan we post weekly, and we would suggest the same rhythm if you do it yourself. Keep each post short, real and specific to something that happened.

## How should you get and answer reviews?

Ask every customer, and reply to every review. Reviews are a major part of what makes a profile look trustworthy to people deciding whom to call.

Make it easy. Send the customer a direct link to your review page by text or email soon after the job, while they remember it well. Then reply to what people write. Thank the happy ones by name and keep replies short. Answer an unhappy review calmly, say what you will do about it, and offer to talk offline.

Follow Google's rules. Do not offer a reward in exchange for a review, and do not ask only the customers you expect to be happy. Do not write reviews for your own business. Google can remove reviews and penalize profiles that break its policies.

## What numbers should you watch?

Your profile reports how many people found it and what they did next. The numbers worth watching are:

- Calls from the profile
- Direction requests
- Website clicks
- Profile views

Compare each with the same month a year earlier, not just with last month. Most local businesses are seasonal, so a drop from August to November may be normal. A drop against last November is worth a look.

## Is your profile consistent with your listings?

The name, address and phone number of your business should be the same everywhere they appear: your website, your profile, and the directories and listing sites that copy business details. Small differences, such as "Street" in one place and "St." in another, or an old phone number on a forgotten listing, add confusion for Google and for customers.

If you have moved, changed your number or rebranded, old listings are usually the culprit. Search for your own name and phone number, and fix what you find. In our Local SEO plan, we build, clean up and keep listings consistent for you.
